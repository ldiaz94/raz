title: Arrays, slices and strings
part: Set
summary: Five in a row, a window onto them, and bytes in UTF-8.


`[5]INT` is an array: five `INT`s in a row, copied when it is assigned. `[]INT` is a slice: a pointer to elements that live elsewhere, and their number, `LEN(s)`. `a[lo..hi]` is the slice of the elements from `lo` up to, but not including, `hi`, and an array goes silently where a slice is wanted.

A string is a slice of bytes, `[]U8`, in UTF-8, and its bytes are constant; a `[8]U8` made from one is a copy. Arrays and slices of integers compare with `=` and `#`; `Str` has the rest: ordering, finding, hashing and reading a number.

A `for` loop takes its bounds once, and never steps past the last: with an empty slice, `0 to LEN(s) - 1` doesn't run at all.
